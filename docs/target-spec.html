01 · The structural problem

One grain mismatch causes every current bug

FPL collapses double-gameweek stats into a single row per player per gameweek, but fixtures are naturally one row per match. Joining the two directly fans out on doubles and vanishes on blanks. Every downstream defect traces back to this.

02 · Principles

Four rules the whole design follows

  1. Grain is declared, never incidental. Every model states its grain in a schema description and enforces it with a uniqueness test. A join that changes grain is a bug unless the model name says otherwise.
  2. Provenance stays separate until deliberately merged. API-sourced and vaastav-sourced rows live in different raw tables and are unioned in staging with a source column that survives to the mart. Trust levels differ; silent mixing hides that.
  3. Player attributes are point-in-time. Price, position, team and set-piece order are as-at-gameweek, never current-state joined across history. This is what the current player_team_corrections seed is manually patching.
  4. Scoring rules are versioned, football is not. Component models train on all ten seasons because goals and assists mean the same thing in 2016 as in 2026. Points do not — defensive_contribution arrived in 2025-26 — so scoring is applied last, from a rules table, never learned.
Why this matters most

Principle 4 is what makes the decomposition architecture pay for itself. A direct points-target regressor is only rule-compatible with 2025-26 onward — roughly 30,000 rows. Modelling components and applying current scoring afterwards keeps all 253,900.

Known gap

Set-piece order history for 2025-26 GW1–30 does not exist in any source. The six archived snapshots span 16 Apr – 8 May only, and vaastav's players_raw.csv is a single end-of-season capture. Treat pre-2026-27 set-piece features as end-of-season constants and exclude them from any point-in-time claim.

05 · Intermediate

Spines and rolling form

Two ideas carry this layer. Spines make absence explicit, so a blank gameweek is a row with fixture_cnt = 0 rather than a missing row. Rolling over appearances replaces rolling over gameweeks, so a player's "last five matches" means five matches they actually played, not five calendar slots.

int_team_gameweek_spineseason × team × gameweekComplete cross join. fixture_cnt 0/1/2, is_blank, is_double, and a correct next_gw_fixture_cnt.
int_team_fixtureteam × fixtureLong form: opponent, home/away, difficulty, goals for/against, kickoff. The unit the simulator consumes.
int_team_formteam × fixtureRolling attack/defence over last N matches, ordered by kickoff. Excludes current row.

Deliberate change

Rolling windows switch from rows between N preceding on gameweek to windows over appearances. A player returning from a five-week injury currently carries an average polluted by five zero rows; under the new definition his form reflects the last five times he was on the pitch, and absence is expressed separately by the availability model.

08 · Decision layer

From projections to a plan

Predicted points are half a decision. The optimizer is a mixed-integer program over a multi-gameweek horizon, consuming fct_player_gameweek_projection and the squad rules already sitting in stg_squad_rules.

  • Objective — maximise expected points over the horizon, less transfer penalties.
  • Constraints — budget 100.0, 15-player squad, 11 playing, max three per club, free transfers and −4 hits, valid formation. All read from stg_squad_rules and raw_game_settings rather than hardcoded.
  • Chips — wildcard, free hit, bench boost, triple captain as binary decisions within their season-half windows, from stg_chips.
  • League-relative mode — when rival picks are known, optimise expected rank rather than expected points. Chasing a leader rewards differentials; protecting a lead rewards template. This is the "win your league" behaviour and it is a different objective, not a filter.

Two horizons, not one

Transfer planning and chip planning have opposite error tolerances, so they get separate passes rather than one compromise horizon.

PassHorizonRationale
transfersH = 5 max_extra_free_transfers = 4, so the bank caps at five. No save-and-spend strategy exists at six weeks that is not already expressible at five. Re-run weekly, so week-5 precision barely matters.
chip timingH = 8–10 Coarser pass, no individual transfers. Scores which gameweek in the window suits bench boost or triple captain from fixture density and projected squad strength. One-shot per season half, so it needs range more than precision.

FPL Review projects 14 gameweeks, but that rests on human-in-the-loop xMins refreshed hourly. Without that input, compounding error past roughly eight weeks is mostly noise.

Serving

Streamlit Community Cloud reading MotherDuck. The weekly job publishes to MotherDuck; the app only ever runs queries, never models.

  • Public page — gameweek projections, captaincy ranked by P(≥10), differentials, fixture ticker.
  • Team-ID page — enter an entry id, fetch picks live from the FPL API, join to stored projections, return transfer and chip recommendations.
  • Cold-start note — an arbitrary visitor's squad is not in the warehouse, so picks are fetched at request time and only projections come from MotherDuck.

10 · Sequence

Build order

  1. Scoring rules seed. Encode 2016-17 → 2026-27 point values. Everything downstream depends on it and it is pure reference data.
  2. Staging rebuild. Source unification and the point-in-time snapshot. Retires player_team_corrections.
  3. Spines and intermediate. Where the grain fix actually lands.
  4. Mart + tests. fct_player_fixture green on grain and reconciliation tests.
  5. Baseline re-scored. Rerun the existing LightGBM on the corrected mart. Establishes whether the bugs were costing real accuracy before the architecture changes underneath it.
  6. Components. Appearance and minutes first — the largest single lever.
  7. Simulation. Dixon-Coles, then the sampling loop.
  8. Optimizer. Single gameweek, then multi-week, then chips, then league-relative.
  9. Streamlit + MotherDuck.
  10. Orchestration. Docker and Airflow last, once there is a stable thing to schedule.
Deadline

Gameweek 1 locks at 17:30 UTC on 21 August 2026. Steps 1–4 are the ones worth having before then; everything after can be built against live data as the season runs. Rival picks for a given gameweek are only fetchable after that gameweek's deadline and cannot be backfilled.
