Revised after review

An earlier version of this page recommended a squad with three unplayable bench slots, a player carrying a 75% injury flag, and a goalkeeper selected against a defence from the same squad. Those were optimiser faults, not model faults, and all three are now constraints: bench places carry real value and a minimum probability of appearing, anyone flagged below 75% is excluded outright, and defensive picks from both sides of one fixture are capped because their clean sheets are mutually exclusive. The squad below is the corrected one.

Read this before acting on it

No match has been played this season, so every form input comes from last season, linked by each player's career-stable FPL code. The overall level is about right, but the spread is compressed: last season's rate separates players far less sharply than live form does. Two consequences — steady clean-sheet defenders are systematically favoured over volatile attackers, and the ordering below is weaker evidence than the same table would be by Gameweek 5. Treat it as a starting point that your own knowledge of transfers, pre-season minutes and new signings should override.

Captaincy: rank by P(haul), not by expected points

The tie breaks on the tail: Haaland reaches ten points 15.6% of the time against Fernandes' 14.2%. If you are chasing in a league, take the tail. If you are protecting a lead, either is fine.

Why this metric

Measured on held-out data, ranking by P(haul) picked a 10-point returner in the top five 14.0% of the time against 10.0% for ranking by expected points. A mean cannot express that difference, which is the main reason this project simulates matches rather than regressing on points. The ± figures above are Monte Carlo standard error: at 8,000 draws anything inside about 0.11 of another player is a coin flip, and the decimals should not be read as a ranking.

How this was produced

  • Appearance — three-class model on the FPL scoring thresholds (no appearance / under 60 / 60+), 84.0% accurate on a held-out season, calibration error ~0.01.
  • Scorelines — Dixon-Coles bivariate Poisson with FPL's difficulty rating as a covariate and a two-year decay half-life. Clean-sheet calibration error 0.017.
  • Player events — six per-90 component models with minutes as a Poisson exposure offset. Bonus is recomputed from the events drawn in each simulation, which is what makes hauls correlate properly.
  • Scoring — applied from a per-season rules table validated by reconstruction against 253,578 historical player-gameweeks with zero error.
  • Squad — mixed-integer program over budget, squad size, formation and the three-per-club limit, solved with HiGHS. Bench places carry 15% of their projected value and a 55% floor on appearing; anyone flagged below 75% fitness is excluded; defensive picks from both sides of one fixture are capped.
  • Uncertainty — every projection carries a Monte Carlo standard error. At 8,000 draws that is around 0.05 for a premium, so gaps under roughly 0.11 are noise.
Known limits

Cold start compresses the ranking, as described at the top, which is why the formation comes out 5-4-1 rather than something more balanced. Pre-season friendlies are not modelled at all — the FPL API does not publish them, so a player who has looked sharp or been played out of position through August is invisible here. Neither are manager changes, tactical shifts, or transfers completed after the last data pull. 22% of the player list has no prior-season history whatsoever, mostly promoted-club squads and overseas signings; those players are under-rated rather than over-rated, so their absence from these tables means little. Nothing accounts for injury news published after generation.
